# ADHD Cognitive Basis

**Purpose**: Explain why each shaping rule exists, so the rules can be applied with judgment instead of copied literally.

Read this only when a rule's rationale, an override tradeoff, or the medical boundary is challenged. It is design rationale, not clinical guidance.

## Boundary first

- This document explains output design. It is not a diagnosis, a screening tool, or treatment advice.
- Using this mode says nothing about whether the reader has ADHD. Many readers use it purely as a density preference.
- Individual assessment belongs to a qualified clinician. Answer informational questions plainly, state the limit in one line, and do not speculate about a specific person.

## Five reading facts behind the rules

1. **Working memory is small.** Anything not on screen is effectively gone. Never ask the reader to "keep in mind" something stated three messages ago.
2. **Knowing the answer is not doing the answer.** The gap between "got it" and "done it" is where work dies. Reduce the friction, not just the word count.
3. **Starting is the hardest step.** The first action must be obvious, small, and doable now.
4. **Time estimates feel uniform.** "A bit of work" and "a few hours" land the same. Only concrete units carry information.
5. **Dopamine is scarce.** Visible progress sustains effort; a buried win does not register as a win.

## Fact to rule mapping

| Fact | Rules it drives | Failure when ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Small working memory | 5 (restate state), 9 (cap lists), 10 (no recap) | The reader re-reads the thread to find where they are |
| Knowing is not doing | 1 (action first), 11 (do the work you own) | A correct answer that never gets executed |
| Starting is hardest | 2 (numbered steps), 3 (one next action) | Task abandoned at the planning stage |
| Uniform time perception | 6 (concrete estimates) | Work is scheduled wrong or never started |
| Scarce dopamine | 7 (make wins visible), 8 (flat error tone) | Progress feels invisible; errors feel like failure |

## Why brevity alone fails

Short output is not the goal. A three-line answer that hides the command, omits the rollback note, or hands the work back to the reader fails on every fact above. The rules are about *ordering and visibility* first, length second. That is why the scoring rubric in [`../rules/validation.md`](../rules/validation.md) weights correctness at 35% and concision at 10%.

## Why the shape survives overrides

Depth, safety confirmations, ranked options, and harness-mandated sections all add words. None of them require preamble, recaps, or closers. The floor holds because the floor is about where the answer sits, not how many words surround it.
